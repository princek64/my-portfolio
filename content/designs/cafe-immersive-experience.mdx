# Cafe Immersive Experience

**Interaction Design · Figma · Miro · ShapesXR · ThingLink · Insta 360 · Meta Quest 3 · 2026**

A project I did for my MA Designing Interaction module at Birkbeck. The idea was to put someone inside a specialty cafe using 360-degree footage, so they could explore the space and learn how a roastery works without being there.

---

## The Concept

I shot 360-degree footage inside a specialty cafe with an in-house roastery, then made it interactive. So it's not a virtual simulation — it's real footage you can move through and click around in.

It sits somewhere between a documentary and an experience. You're not watching a video. You're standing in the room.

---

## Tools

- **Insta 360** for the footage
- **ThingLink** to add interactive hotspots to the 360 environment
- **ShapesXR** for spatial prototyping in VR
- **Meta Quest 3** for testing in headset
- **Miro** for journey mapping and content planning
- **Figma** for any screen-based UI components

---

## The Design Problem

How do you guide someone through a space when they can look anywhere?

With a flat UI, you use hierarchy and layout. In 360, none of that applies. I had to think differently about where to place prompts, when to surface information, and how long to let someone sit in a space before nudging them somewhere else.

---

## What I Took Away

Using this many tools in one project is messy. Each one has its own logic, and getting them to play nicely together took more planning than I expected. Spending time in the headset testing the experience made it obvious how different spatial interaction is from screen design. Small things that seem fine on a monitor feel completely wrong when you're wearing a Quest.

The written reflection that came with the project helped me make sense of those decisions, which ended up being just as useful as the experience itself.
