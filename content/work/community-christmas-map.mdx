Community Christmas helps people who'd otherwise spend Christmas Day alone find a lunch, a carol service or just some company near them. I designed and built it for Marmalade Trust, the loneliness charity, as a volunteer with Scottish Tech Army. It went live in November 2026.

## The problem

Every year, churches, councils, cafés and community groups put on Christmas Day events. They're listed on noticeboards, in parish newsletters and in Facebook groups, if they're listed at all. The people who'd most like to go are often older, often on a phone, and often the least likely to find them.

Marmalade Trust wanted one place to find them all, run by a small team who already keep their events in a Google Sheet.

## What I made

For visitors, it's a search box. Type a postcode or a town, and you get the events nearby as a list, with a map one tap away.

For hosts, it's a short form. Five steps, no account to create, and a private link to change or withdraw the event later.

For the charity's staff, it's a portal. Nothing goes live until they approve it, and the first thing they see is what needs doing: new events, possible duplicates and events missing accessibility details.

## Designing for older eyes

The person reading might be 80, on an old phone, without their glasses. So the facts that decide whether someone goes, like when, where, cost and step-free access, are large, bold and dark. Grey is kept for things you can skip. Every button has a clear edge, so it looks like something you can press.

The site also never guesses. If a host hasn't said whether an event is free or step-free, it shows nothing rather than a hopeful badge. Turning up to find steps you can't manage is worse than not knowing.

## Things we learned after launch

Analytics showed that many visitors were on computers, where a "Call" button does nothing. Phone numbers can now be copied as well as called.

On a weak signal, part of the map could fail to load and take the pins with it. The map was rebuilt so the pins always show, and nearby pins join into numbered bubbles that open up as you zoom in.

In testing, staff saw a plain text box where hosts had a set of tick boxes, because the two forms had drifted apart. Now the host form and the staff form share one set of questions.

## A bit of Christmas

The site is for one day of the year, so it should feel like it. On Christmas Day it says so, lights and snow come out, and events read "Today" or "On now". When a search finds nothing nearby, there's a short Christmas game to play while you wait, and a way to tell the charity where events are needed.

## Where it is now

Live at [communitychristmas.org.uk](https://www.communitychristmas.org.uk), with hosts adding events ahead of 25 December. The next step is moving the events from the Google Sheet into the charity's HubSpot, so staff can run everything from one place.
